IEEE 738 Thermal Pipeline

From one line in Virginia to every conductor's temperature and thermal cycles.

The pipeline estimates the temperature of every overhead transmission conductor in a network, hour by hour, with the IEEE Std 738 heat balance, and counts the thermal cycles each one goes through. Step 1 gathers the inputs: the conductor and its structures, the nearest NOAA weather station, and the amperage from the busier of the two substations at the line's ends. Step 2 calculates. Pipeline steps

    How it works

    Step 1 gathers the inputs

    Each conductor's metadata (type, diameter, resistance, emissivity and absorptivity, its maximum operating temperature) is joined to the structures that carry it, span by span. The nearest NOAA station supplies the weather the heat balance needs: sunlight, whether the air is clear or industrial, wind speed and direction, and air temperature. Wind decides the convection model: with wind the conductor is cooled by forced convection; in still air, by natural convection. A line has a substation at each end; the pipeline maps both to the conductor and uses the amperage from the one whose readings vary most, the higher standard deviation σ = √(Σ(x − x̄)² / n), because a flat series can hide the load swings that heat and cool the line.

    Step 2 calculates temperatures and thermal cycles

    Step 2 runs every substep in turn. A flight check confirms the metadata, weather and amperage cover the same dates. Then the pipeline solves the IEEE 738 heat balance for every conductor and hour: the heat the conductor sheds by convection and radiation equals the heat it gains from the sun and from its own resistance, qc + qr = qs + I²R(Tc).

    Thermal cycles

    A thermal cycle is an up event, a rise of 35 °C from the coolest point since the last down event, followed by a down event, a fall of 35 °C from the hottest point since the last up event. Smaller swings don't count. A run rarely starts or ends at a clean break, so the historian keeps the half cycle a run ends in, and the next run starts from it: thermal cycle stitching.

    Optional steps

    Steps 4, 6 and 7 are for validation: CSV copies of the results, a dashboard of the temperatures, and the run's resource metrics. Step 5 conforms each config to its .dist template: global_config.yaml.dist → global_config.yaml, prod_config.yaml.dist → prod_config.yaml and batch_config.yaml.dist → batch_config.yaml.

    The animation's numbers are illustrative: one conductor over 100 hours, with a one-line stand-in for the heat balance. The pipeline itself solves the full IEEE Std 738 balance for every reading.
